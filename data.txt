Pakistan’s nuclear program began in 1956 with the establishment of the Pakistan
 Atomic Energy Commission (PAEC) to conduct research for peaceful nuclear energy. Initially, the program relied on technical
 assistance from the Atoms for Peace initiative of the United States. The 1971 war
  and India’s 1974 Smiling Buddha nuclear test prompted Pakistan to accelerate its nuclear efforts.
 Prime Minister Zulfikar Ali
 Bhutto officially approved the program during the 1972 Multan Conference, declaring
  that Pakistan
 would develop nuclear weapons under any circumstances. In 1976, Dr. Abdul Qadeer Khan
  joined the
 program and established the Kahuta Research Laboratories (KRL), using gas centrifuge
  technology for uranium enrichment. Dr. A.Q. Khan is
 widely recognized as the Father of Pakistan’s Nuclear Program.
Despite international pressure and sanctions, including the 1990 Pressler Amendment,
 Pakistan kept its program secret. Eventually, Pakistan conducted its first nuclear test, Chagai-I, on 28 May 1998,
 followed by Chagai-II on 30 May 1998 in Chagai Hills, Balochistan. With these tests,
  Pakistan became the 7th nuclear power in the world. The main purpose of Pakistan’s nuclear
 program ha
 s been national security,
 
 particularly to balance India’s nuclear threat, with a defensive nuclear doctrine.
  The key institutions
 involved in the program include PAEC, KRL, and the Strategic Plans Division (SPD),
  which oversees Pakistan’s nuclear assets.
